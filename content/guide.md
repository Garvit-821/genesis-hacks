# Introduction

## How to run a hackathon: a practical guide

`/guide` · nav: Overview · What this guide covers and how to use it · updated October 2026

**Planning, funding and running a student hackathon in India**

Whether you are running a 100-person campus event or a 1,000-person weekend, this guide walks through the timeline, the budget, sponsors, the venue and the day itself, with checklists you can copy.

### What is this guide?

Organising a hackathon has a lot of moving parts: venue access, sponsors, food, hardware, judging and participant safety. This guide puts them in order, step by step, so a first-time team knows what to do and when.

> **Pro Tip:** Share this guide with your co-organisers at your first planning meeting.

## What is a Hackathon?

`/guide/introduction/what-is-a-hackathon` · nav:  · What happens at a hackathon and who it is for · updated October 2026

**An invention marathon where passion meets rapid prototyping**

A hackathon is an event where people team up to build something in a fixed time, usually 24 to 48 hours, then show it to judges and each other.

### The Core Philosophy

The point is to learn by building: try a new stack, ship a working demo in a weekend, and meet people you would want to build with again. Prizes are a bonus.

### Who attends hackathons?

You don't need to be a Computer Science major or a veteran developer! Great teams combine programmers, UI/UX designers, hardware tinkerers, project strategists, and domain specialists.

> **Plan for first-timers:** Many participants at student hackathons are attending their first one. Beginner workshops and a beginner track help them finish something.

## Community Values

`/guide/introduction/community-values` · nav:  · Safety, inclusion, and the hacker code of conduct · updated October 2026

**A safe, welcoming event for everyone**

Publish a code of conduct and enforce it. Everyone should be free from harassment regardless of gender, sexual orientation, disability, ethnicity or background.

### Enforcing the Code of Conduct

Link the code of conduct from your website, put posters up at check-in, and name one organiser as the incident lead who can be reached at any hour of the event.

> **Safety requirement:** Provide an anonymous way to report problems and a direct emergency phone number during the event.

# Event Types

## Digital Events

`/guide/event-types/digital-events` · nav:  · Hosting virtual and online hackathons · updated October 2026

**Running a hackathon online**

Online hackathons let people join from anywhere. Teams collaborate over chat and video, and submit online.

### Recommended Digital Tech Stack

Use Discord for real-time team matching and voice channels, YouTube/Twitch for keynotes and ceremonies, and Devpost/HackCulture for project submissions.

### Keeping Digital Engagement High

Online events lose people fast. Run short 15-minute mini-events (trivia, games, quizzes) and keep mentor voice channels staffed.

## In-Person Events

`/guide/event-types/in-person-events` · nav:  · Running a 24 to 48 hour event at a venue · updated October 2026

**What a venue needs**

In-person events need more planning than online ones, and they are where teams form and people come back next year.

### Venue Essentials

Confirm overnight building access, reliable Wi-Fi, at least one power socket per participant, accessible restrooms and a quiet rest area.

## Hybrid Events

`/guide/event-types/hybrid-events` · nav:  · Combining physical venues with online participation · updated October 2026

**The best of both worlds with dual submission tracks**

Hybrid hackathons allow local participants to hack in person while remote participants submit and present virtually.

### Ensuring Judging Fairness

Run separate judging queues or virtual video rooms to ensure remote teams receive equal evaluation time compared to floor expo tables.

## Finding the Date & Purpose

`/guide/general-information/finding-date-and-purpose` · nav:  · Avoiding scheduling conflicts and defining your theme · updated October 2026

**Strategic scheduling for maximum attendance**

Choosing the wrong date can destroy attendance. Cross-check university exam schedules, major holidays, and competing regional tech events before locking in your date.

### Date Selection Checklist

Check academic calendars for midterms and finals, national holidays, local weather patterns, and major tech conference dates.

## Build Your Leadership Team

`/guide/general-information/build-leadership-team` · nav:  · Structuring functional organising committees · updated October 2026

**Give each lead clear ownership**

Split the work into clear areas, each with one person responsible.

### Core organising areas

1. Lead Director (Overall vision & sync)
2. Logistics Director (Venue, catering, AV, security)
3. Sponsorship Lead (Funding, partner relations)
4. Tech & Web Lead (Platform, registration system, Wi-Fi)
5. Marketing & Design Lead (Socials, branding, swag)
6. Hacker Experience Lead (Mentors, mini-events, judging)

## Locking Down a Venue

`/guide/general-information/locking-down-a-venue` · nav:  · Facility requirements, contracts, and power setup · updated October 2026

**Finding and booking a venue**

Check overnight access approvals, ventilation and power before you sign anything.

### Power & Gigabit Networking

A normal classroom cannot power 50 laptops from one wall. Work with the venue's facilities team on distribution boards and cables across several circuits.

> **Power Rule of Thumb:** Plan for about 150 W per participant. A 300-person hackathon needs roughly 45 kW of continuous power.

## Hackathon Budgeting

`/guide/general-information/hackathon-budgeting` · nav:  · Financial planning, line items, and reserve funds · updated October 2026

**Planning income and costs**

A budget agreed early stops you running out of food or money on the day. These are typical shares; adjust them for your event.

### Standard Expense Allocations

- Food & Beverage: 40-45%
- Swag & Shirts: 15-20%
- Travel & Logistics: 15%
- Prizes & Hardware: 10%
- Audio/Visual & Security: 10%
- Emergency Reserve: 5-10%

# Getting Sponsorship

## Sponsorship Overview

`/guide/getting-sponsorship/sponsorship-overview` · nav:  · Fundraising strategy and tier structures · updated October 2026

**Why companies sponsor hackathons**

Sponsors fund your event to recruit top talent, drive developer adoption of their APIs/SDKs, and build brand affinity among student creators.

### What Sponsors Care About

Tailor your pitch to what company representatives care about: HR teams want resumes and interview access; Developer Relations teams want API usage and project submissions.

## Prospectus Creation

`/guide/getting-sponsorship/prospectus-creation` · nav:  · What to put in a sponsorship deck · updated October 2026

**Crafting a clean, compelling 3-page proposal**

Keep the deck short and specific: who comes, what a sponsor gets, and what it costs.

### Essential Deck Sections

1. Cover & Vision
2. Key Demographics & Past Statistics (Hackers count, schools, diversity, projects built)
3. Tier Comparison Table (Title, Gold, Silver, Bronze)
4. Custom Add-ons (Meal sponsorship, Hardware lab, Lounge naming)
5. Contact & Payment Info

## Pitching Sponsors

`/guide/getting-sponsorship/pitching-sponsors` · nav:  · Outreach campaigns, follow-ups, and closing contracts · updated October 2026

**Outreach, follow-ups and closing**

Start your outreach 4 to 6 months in advance. Target University Recruiter and Developer Advocate titles on LinkedIn.

### Outreach Best Practices

Keep initial cold emails under 150 words. Highlight your expected hacker headcount, university reach, and unique track opportunities.

# Hackathon Website

## Making an Accessible Design

`/guide/hackathon-website/accessible-design` · nav:  · WCAG compliance, mobile UX, and high contrast · updated October 2026

**Designing for every hacker on all screen sizes**

Your website is often the first thing participants and sponsors see. Check colour contrast, labels, screen reader support and keyboard navigation.

### Accessibility Checklist

Maintain minimum 4.5:1 text contrast ratio, provide alt text on all images, ensure all buttons have accessible labels, and support keyboard tab navigation.

### Teaser Elements

Hero headline, tentative date and city, email newsletter subscribe form, sponsor outreach contact link.

# Event Logistics

## Managing Registrations

`/guide/event-logistics/managing-registrations` · nav:  · Applications, ticketing, RSVPs, and check-in flows · updated October 2026

**Streamlining application reviews and attendance confirmation**

Free events usually see a share of registered people not turn up, so accept more registrations than you have seats. Track your own drop-off rate to set the margin.

### RSVP Strategy

Send acceptance notifications in waves. Require hackers to confirm RSVPs 7 days before the event so waitlisted applicants can be promoted.

## Judging & Submissions

`/guide/event-logistics/judging-and-submissions` · nav:  · Expo style judging, scoring rubrics, and platforms · updated October 2026

**Fair, transparent, and efficient project evaluations**

For large hackathons, expo-style judging (teams demo at their tables) lets every team show their project to several judges, which stage presentations cannot.

### Standard Judging Rubric

1. Technical Complexity (25%)
2. Design & User Experience (25%)
3. Originality & Innovation (25%)
4. Utility & Impact (25%)

## Hackathon Scheduling

**Balancing hacking time, workshops, and rest**

## Mentorship

`/guide/event-logistics/mentorship` · nav:  · Onboarding and organising technical mentors · updated October 2026

**Getting teams unstuck**

Recruit experienced engineers, alumni and sponsor developer advocates to help teams when they are stuck.

### Help Desk Ticketing

Use a ticket queue (a Discord bot or a simple web form) where teams give their table number, stack and problem, so mentors can pick up requests in order.

## Day-of Logistics & Hacker Experience

`/guide/event-logistics/day-of-logistics` · nav:  · On-site operations, meals, swag, and emergency response · updated October 2026

**Running the day itself**

Write the run of show minute by minute, and give core staff walkie-talkies or a dedicated emergency chat channel.

### Hacker Experience Essentials

Keep water stations full, maintain clean restrooms, hand out snacks every 4 hours, and provide earplugs/sleeping masks in designated rest areas.

## After the Event

`/guide/event-logistics/after-the-event` · nav:  · Post-mortems, metrics, winner follow-ups, and thank-yous · updated October 2026

**Closing the loop and building community momentum**

After the closing ceremony, thank sponsors, pay out prizes promptly and hold a team retrospective.

### Post-Event Checklist

Send attendee survey, publish winner project showcase, distribute tax receipts to sponsors, process reimbursements, and write an internal retrospective document.

# Hardware Hackathon Guide

## Hardware Introduction

`/guide/hardware-guide/hardware-intro` · nav:  · Why host hardware & IoT hackathons? · updated October 2026

**Combining physical electronics with software intelligence**

Hardware hackathons allow participants to build physical gadgets, robotics, wearables, and smart home IoT systems using microcontrollers and sensors.

### What Makes Hardware Special

Hardware hackers need physical workbenches, soldering stations, component check-out counters, 3D printers, and safety gear.

## Hardware Labs & Setup

`/guide/hardware-guide/hardware-labs-resources` · nav:  · Equipment inventories, component check-out, and safety · updated October 2026

**Setting up a safe hardware lab**

Provide a diverse inventory of microcontrollers (Arduino, Raspberry Pi, ESP32), sensor kits, wire strippers, breadboards, and soldering equipment.

### Mandatory Safety Rules

Always require safety goggles in soldering areas, provide fume extractors, maintain a first aid burn kit, and prohibit high-voltage main grid hacking without approval.

> **Safety First:** Have a trained lab manager supervise soldering stations at all times.

# Organiser Resources

## Templates to make

`/guide/organizer-resources/template-links` · nav:  · The planning documents every organising team needs · updated October 2026

**Five documents to set up before you start**

Most teams end up building the same few documents. Set them up early and share them with every lead.

### What to set up

- Budget sheet: every income and cost line, with a reserve
- Sponsorship deck: audience, packages and contact
- Run of show: the event minute by minute, with owners
- Volunteer roster: shifts and responsibilities
- Incident log: what happened, when, and what was done

## Host Exciting Mini-Events

`/guide/organizer-resources/host-exciting-mini-events` · nav:  · Cup stacking, Slides Karaoke, Esports, and trivia ideas · updated October 2026

**Short breaks that keep people going**

Mini-events break up intense coding sessions, relieve stress, and create fun memories.

### Ideas that work

1. Cup Stacking Challenge (Fastest team to build a 50-cup pyramid)
2. Slides Karaoke (Hackers present random 5-slide decks they've never seen before)
3. Midnight Cupcake Decorating
4. Smash Bros / Valorant Tournament
5. Tech Trivia & Meme Contest
