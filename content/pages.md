## How we run events.
The rules we hold ourselves to when we run a hackathon or partner on one. Hold us to them.

### Our six rules

#### Working builds over slides
Judges at events we run score what a team built during the event. A demo that works beats a polished pitch deck.

#### Build in public
We encourage teams to share their code, write up what broke, and keep their projects online after the event.

#### Open to anyone who builds
You do not need a computer science degree or a famous college. First-timers, designers and self-taught developers are welcome at every event we run.

#### Numbers we can back up
We only publish figures we can source, and we say when a number is a projection rather than a result.

#### Help each other
Mentors, organisers and other teams are there to get you unstuck. Asking early is encouraged.

#### Leave it better
We respect venues, mentors' time and other people's work, and we clean up after ourselves.
At every event

### Code of conduct

#### Credit other people's work
Name the libraries, APIs and teammates you used. Submitting someone else's work as your own is not allowed.

#### Critique the work, not the person
Challenge ideas and code directly, and stay kind to the people behind them.

#### Say when you do not know
Nobody knows everything at 3 AM. Asking is how teams finish.

#### No harassment, ever
Harassment, discrimination and gatekeeping are not tolerated at any Genesis event, online or offline.

#### Report a problem
At an event, tell any organiser straight away. Afterwards, email

We collect what you type into our forms so we can reply to you, we never sell it, analytics stay off unless you accept them, and you can ask us to see, correct or delete your data at any time by writing to

#### Collected automatically
- Security and spam control. When you send a form we store a one-way, salted hash of your IP address and your browser’s user agent, so we can block repeated or automated submissions. We cannot turn the hash back into your IP address.
- Server logs. Our hosting provider keeps short-lived technical logs (IP address, time, page requested) to run and protect the site.
- Analytics, only with your consent. If you click Accept on the cookie banner, PostHog records the pages you visit, device type, approximate region and where you came from. If you click Reject, or do nothing, no analytics load.
We do not ask for sensitive information such as financial details, health data or identity documents through this site. Please do not include them in a message or upload.

### Why we use it, and on what basis
We use your personal data to:
- reply to your message or enquiry and keep the conversation going;
- assess sponsorship, partnership and collaboration requests and match them to events;
- tell you about the event you registered interest in, such as Hackers Occupied Delhi;
- consider crew applications;
- issue certificates to registered participants;
- keep the site secure and free of spam; and
- with your consent, understand which pages are useful.
Consent. Sending a form is a clear, affirmative act: you give us your data for the purpose shown on that form. Analytics run only on your separate consent through the cookie banner.
Legitimate uses. Some processing relies on the legitimate uses allowed by section 7 of the DPDP Act, for example using data you gave us voluntarily for the purpose you gave it, or keeping records a law requires us to keep.
We do not sell personal data, we do not use it for advertising, and we do not make automated decisions about you.

### How long we keep it
- Enquiries (contact, partner, collaborate, crew applications, event interest): up to 24 months after our last exchange with you, then deleted.
- Uploaded decks and files: up to 12 months, then deleted.
- Certificate checks: not stored. The participant list itself is kept as one-way hashes of each registered email and phone pair.
- Analytics: kept by PostHog in line with its retention settings, and only if you accepted.
We delete data sooner if you ask us to and no law requires us to keep it, or once the purpose you gave it for is complete.

- have a grievance about our handling of your data addressed; and
- nominate another person to exercise these rights for you if you die or become unable to.

### Withdrawing consent and cookies
Withdrawing consent is as easy as giving it. Use Cookie settings at the bottom of any page to change your analytics choice, or email us to withdraw consent for anything else. Withdrawal does not affect processing that already happened.
The site stores a few small items in your browser:
- Your consent choice (essential), so we remember whether you accepted or rejected analytics.
- Intro seen (essential, this browser session only), so the opening animation plays once.
- PostHog cookies and storage, only after you click Accept.

### Children’s data
The DPDP Act treats anyone under 18 as a child, and processing a child’s data needs verifiable consent from a parent or lawful guardian.
- The forms on this site are meant for adults. If you are under 18, please ask a parent or guardian to contact us for you.
- Registration for student events such as Hackers Occupied needs verifiable consent from a parent or guardian for any participant under 18.
- We do not track children, monitor their behaviour or show them targeted advertising.
- If we learn that we hold a child’s data without that consent, we delete it. Parents and guardians can write to [partner@genesishacks.com](mailto:partner@genesishacks.com) at any time.

### How we protect it
- The whole site runs over HTTPS.
- Form submissions and decks are stored in a private database and a private storage bucket that the public cannot read. Only our server functions hold the access key, and it never reaches your browser.
- IP addresses are stored only as salted hashes.
- Access to submissions is limited to the Genesis team members who need it.
No system is perfectly secure. If a breach affects your personal data, we will inform you and the Data Protection Board of India as the DPDP Act requires.
